📊 Click to view Native Forest Loss Statistics ▼

Prior to human settlement, native forests covered over 85% of New Zealand's total land area. Today, after centuries of logging, agricultural clearing, and land development, less than 24% of original native forest remains. Lowland forests have been particularly devastated, with over 90% cleared in populated regions like Auckland.

.Native podocarp forests (such as Kauri, Rimu, and Kahikatea) are extremely powerful carbon sinks, absorbing up to 150 tonnes of carbon per hectare over their lifespan. Restoring just 1 hectare of native bush filters millions of liters of stormwater runoff and prevents hundreds of tonnes of topsoil from eroding into local rivers each year.

1. Ancient Canopy (The Past)

For millions of years, New Zealand evolved in geographic isolation, resulting in ancient Gondwanan rainforests dominated by massive podocarps like Kauri, Tōtara, and Rimu. These untouched canopies housed unique flightless bird species, rich moss layers, and dense understories that naturally regulated the climate and stabilized the soil.

2. Deforestation & Today's Crisis

Widespread clearing for agriculture, commercial timber, and urban expansion dramatically fragmented native bush. Combined with introduced pests—such as brushtail possums, which consume tons of native foliage nightly—our remaining forests face severe dieback (including Kauri Dieback disease) and severe habitat loss for native fauna.

3. Restoring the Balance (Our Future)

Kaitiakitanga, we can halt environmental decline. By protecting existing old-growth remnants, trapping invasive pests, and establishing continuous native planting corridors, communities can reconnect fragmented bush reserves and restore natural ecological balance across New Zealand.
